# 600 mm folding quadcopter airframe, v1

Original design, generated by `generate_frame.py`. It takes its requirements from the reference video (600 mm size, foldable, about 100 mm or more of ground clearance, hollow arms) but none of its geometry.

**Status: not yet printed or flown.** The checks below are geometry checks and hand estimates. They don't replace the physical tests in this file.

## Regenerate

```
pip install manifold3d trimesh shapely numpy matplotlib pillow
python generate_frame.py
```

Change the values in the `PARAMETERS` block at the top of the script. The checks run again each time.

## Parts to print

All parts fit the Ender 3 V3 SE bed (220 x 220 mm) and need no supports. The STLs are already turned to fit, so don't rotate them in the slicer.

| STL | Qty | Size (mm) | Put on the bed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `arm_x4.stl` | 4 | 183 x 183 x 30 (diagonal) | Motor face down |
| `top_plate_x1.stl` | 1 | 201 x 201 x 4 | Flat |
| `bottom_plate_x1.stl` | 1 | 201 x 201 x 34 | Walls up |
| `landing_hoop_x2.stl` | 2 | 205 x 185 x 24 | Web flat, flange up |
| `canopy_x1.stl` | 1 | 174 x 114 x 50 | Open side down |

## Slicer settings

| Setting | Arms | Other parts |
|---|---|---|
| Material | PETG | PETG (canopy can be PLA) |
| Layer height | 0.2 mm | 0.2 mm |
| Walls | 6 lines | 4 lines |
| Top and bottom layers | 6 | 5 |
| Infill | 30%, gyroid | 30%, gyroid |
| Supports | Off | Off |
| Bed adhesion | 8 mm brim (use glue on the bed) | Brim only if corners lift |

Check that the brim still fits on the bed. If it runs off the edge, shrink it.

Avoid PLA for the arms: it softens around 55-60 °C, and motors and hot sun can get close to that.

## Fasteners

| Use | Hardware | Qty |
|---|---|---|
| Arm pivot and arm lock | M4 x 50 + washers + nyloc nuts | 8 |
| Plate bolts (6 of them) | M3 x 45 + nyloc nuts | 6 |
| Plate and canopy bolts (corners) | M3 x 50 + nyloc nuts | 4 |
| Landing hoops | M3 x 16 + washers + nyloc nuts | 4 |
| Motors | M3 x 10 (4 per motor) | 16 |

The motor pad is 6 mm thick, so an M3 x 10 goes about 4 mm into the motor. Check that this stops short of the windings before tightening.

## Assembly order

1. Test-fit a bolt in every hole. Clear out holes with a 3.4 mm or 4.3 mm drill by hand if tight.
2. Bolt the landing hoops under the bottom plate, flanges pointing inward.
3. Fit the motors, then pass the motor wires through the hole by the motor and along the hollow arm. They come out of the diamond-shaped side opening near the root.
4. Put the arm roots on the bottom plate, lay the top plate on, and fit the M4 bolts and the M3 plate bolts.
5. Mount the flight controller on the top plate. It has 30.5 mm and 45 mm hole patterns.
6. Bolt the canopy on with the four corner bolts.

To fold: remove the lock bolt on each arm (the outer one) and swing the arm. Front arms swing back to 160° and rear arms swing back to 180°. Take the props off or use folding props.

## Check before printing the full set

- **Motor bolt pattern:** the arm has both 25 x 25 mm and 30 x 30 mm patterns, but these are assumed values. Measure your motor. If it differs, change `MOTOR_PATTERNS`.
- **Motor and prop size:** the arm and clearance checks assume a motor up to 58 mm wide and 12 inch props.
- **Battery:** the bottom plate's strap slots and the 56 mm opening between the hoops are sized for a 6S 4200 mAh pack about 50 mm wide. Measure yours.
- **NIDAR limits:** I could not read the official rulebook, so nothing here has been checked against it. Confirm the size, weight, guard and frame-origin rules for your track.

## Check results and limits

Run `python generate_frame.py` for the current results. In v1, all checks pass:

- All five STLs are watertight when re-read from disk, and fit the bed.
- Arms and motors don't collide with each other or the frame, deployed or folded.
- Each arm clears the frame at 25 positions through its fold swing. Arm-to-arm contact is only checked at the deployed and folded end positions, so fold the rear arms first and watch the first fold.
- 12 inch props are 119 mm apart tip to tip and clear the canopy.

Estimates (2 kg thrust per motor and PETG properties, both assumed):

| Value | Result |
|---|---|
| Arm tip deflection | 0.60 mm |
| Peak arm bending stress | 1.5 MPa (safety factor 20) |
| Arm bending frequency | 74 Hz |
| Printed mass | Under 1221 g (this figure assumes solid parts, so real parts are lighter) |
| Size, deployed | 482 x 482 x 218 mm without props |
| Size, folded | 400 x 335 x 218 mm without props |

These estimates cover smooth arm bending only. They leave out layer adhesion, bolt holes, the diamond openings, crash loads and fatigue. The weak points are more likely to be the arm root, the hoop flanges and the layers themselves.

## Test before flying

1. **Arm load test:** clamp a bare arm by its root and hang 4 kg from the motor end for 5 minutes (twice the assumed thrust). Look for cracks and permanent bend.
2. **Landing gear test:** assemble the frame at full flight weight and press down firmly, then drop it from 100 mm. Inspect the hoop flanges.
3. **Motor test:** with the props off, run each motor, then check for loose bolts.
4. **Tethered hover:** do the first flight tethered, in an open space, with a tested failsafe and nobody nearby. Check the flight controller's vibration logs.

## Not in v1

Motor and prop guards, payload release, camera gimbal, GPS mast, ESC mounts, LED mounts and battery tray. Add these once the NIDAR track is known.
