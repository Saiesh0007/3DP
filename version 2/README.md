# NIDAR AirMouse frame, version 2

Original design for Mission 2 (AirMouse), generated by `generate_frame.py`. Arms fold downward. It is built around a Pixhawk 2.4.8, Raspberry Pi 4B, YDLidar X2, TFmini-S, PX4Flow, Readytosky 2212 920 KV motors, 40 A ESCs and a CNHL 3S 5000 mAh battery.

**Status: not yet printed or flown.** The checks below cover geometry and estimates only. Do the thrust test first: it decides whether this motor and battery setup can carry the drone.

Other rules to plan for, which a frame can't solve on its own:

- **Live camera feed (8.27):** your parts list has no camera. The frame has a mount for a Raspberry Pi camera board.
- **Emergency stop and failsafes (8.31):** set these up in the flight software and test them.
- **Fast Completion bonus (25 marks):** the mission must finish within 15 minutes, and the estimated flight time here is about 8.

## Why it can't be much smaller

At 3S, 2212 920 KV motors need 10-inch props to produce useful thrust, and smaller props lift less. Each guard needs 8 mm of clearance around the prop, and the guards can't overlap, so that sets the minimum size.

| | Width, square on | Width at worst yaw | Clearance per side at worst yaw |
|---|---|---|---|
| This frame, 10 in props | 560 mm | 677 mm | 161 mm |
| F450 with the same guards (calculated) | 597 mm | 729 mm | 136 mm |

## The main risk: thrust

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Printed frame | About 730 g (880 g at most) |
| Your parts, wiring, bolts | About 1470 g |
| All-up weight | About 2.2 kg |
| Thrust-to-weight | **1.17 to 1.55** (aim for 2.0; below about 1.5 is risky) |
| Hover flight time | **About 8 minutes** (80% of battery used) |

The thrust range comes from published figures for this motor size that disagree: a test table gives 642 g per motor on a 1045 prop at 11.1 V, and seller listings claim 850 g or more. The Readytosky motors have no maker's test data that I could find.

**Test before printing the full set.** Measure one motor with a 1045 prop, your ESC and a charged battery, using a kitchen scale and a secured motor. Keep clear of the prop.

| Measured thrust per motor | What it means |
|---|---|
| Under 700 g | Thrust-to-weight is under about 1.3. Don't fly this setup. Change the motors or battery |
| 700-850 g | Marginal. Fly slowly and cut weight |
| Over 850 g | Thrust-to-weight is at least about 1.55. Usable indoors, and more is better |

Ways to improve it:

1. Use stronger motors or a higher-voltage battery. This has the biggest effect, and 4S with smaller props could also shrink the frame, but needs its own thrust test.
2. Weigh your lidar: the datasheet says 200 g, while another listing says 126 g.
3. Drop the upper deck height (`POST_H`) if your wiring allows.

Enter the true sensor positions in the flight controller settings, because the sensors sit off-centre. The TFmini-S can't read below 10 cm, and sits about 54 mm off the ground when landed, so its reading is invalid until the drone climbs. Set the flight controller's minimum-range setting to match.

## Folding

1. Remove the lock bolt on each arm (the upper, outer bolt). Leave the hinge bolt in.
2. Swing the arm down.
3. To open, swing it up until it stops against the wall, then refit the lock bolt.

Folded size is 554 × 272 × 312 mm. Leave enough wire slack at each hinge for 90° of movement, or the wires will fatigue. Check all four lock bolts before every flight.

Thrust pushes the arm against a solid stop wall, so the lock bolt doesn't carry flight loads. It stops the arm folding on the ground or in a bump.

## Check results

Run `python generate_frame.py` for current results. All checks pass:

- All 10 STLs are watertight when re-read from disk, and fit the bed.
- Arms, guards and motors clear each other, the body and the legs, deployed, folded and at 9 positions through the fold with all four arms moving together.
- The arm can't rotate upward past its stop wall.
- 10 inch props clear the guards, arms and body.
- The X2 scan plane is above the guards and motors, and the downward sensors see past the legs.
- Hinge, leg, deck, sensor, camera and guard bolt holes line up.

Not modelled or tested: real strength, layer adhesion, vibration, crash loads, airflow under the deck, and thrust lost near the guards. The camera lens view, wiring routes and bolt heads aren't checked either.

## Test order

1. Thrust test on one motor (see above).
2. Print one arm A, one guard set and the bottom deck. Check fit, hinge motion and lock bolt.
3. Clamp the deck and hang 2 kg from the motor pad for 5 minutes. Look for cracks or bending.
4. Print the rest. Weigh everything and update `MASSES`.
5. Run motors with props off, and check for loose bolts.
6. Tie the drone down loosely for a first hover test in an open space with a tested emergency stop. Check the vibration logs, especially for the X2 on its tall posts.

Rule 8.5 bans tethers during the mission itself; a tether is only for your own testing.
